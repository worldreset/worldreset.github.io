Michael Wise Memorial Tournament

Sept 26, 2025

I had an insane experience playing in the Michael Wise Memorial tournament. It took place in Toronto, two weeks ago, with fourteen games over two days. There was a class prize to be claimed, issued to the highest finisher among the bottom-half seeded players in each division. I had figured that was my best shot at winning a cash prize. Thus, the games against the players I have highlighted below were critical for me to win. Would I be able to do it?



Chapter 1 - I Did it for the Love of the Game

For anyone who wants to pick apart my games or if you prefer to follow along visually, here is the link. I love to talk about scrabble, send me an email or add me on discord if you have any comments or if you wanna send me a picture of a cat or something. I felt confident going into my first game, facing William Barfuss. On my opening turn, I spotted my only bingo of EREMIT(I)C (pertaining to eremites; a recluse or hermit). A few turns later, I found yet again my only playable bingo of SUNCARE. I continued making strong plays until disaster struck. I was down to just minutes on my clock entering the endgame, ahead by 24 points. I began to feel uneasy with the time I had left to work with. I was behind on tile tracking and did not realize that William had the insanely strong rack of EINOPRS! Instead of finishing my tracking which would only have taken 30 extra seconds, I played my top equity move of RUSTIE(R). This was a game losing blunder. William pounced on the opportunity, spotting his only winning play of (T)ROPINES (a poisonous alkaloid) to win with a final score of 364-338. Had I played slower, I would have almost certainly won the game. Really wanted to rip my hair out after this one.


In my next game against Christopher Sykes, I opened with my best play of FOEHN. I drew into the clunky CLLORTV after Christophers bingo of OUTSW(O)RN, and was forced to play (S)CROLL for only 16 points. Later in the game holding another janky combination of LNOPTTU, I played the phony UN(C)LOT. Christopher was having none of it and immediately vanquished the play off the board. I continued to draw into mediocre to bad racks, until I was down by 114 points. On tilt, I spotted DAIKON as my top play but began to doubt its validity. Instead I played OKA, a significant mistake. Christopher then bingoed with MYNHEER (a Dutch title of courtesy) hooking his Y onto my FAULT to make (FAULT)Y. Now I'm down by 180, heck. I bingoed with TRIUNES (a group of three) to raise my score to a paltry 265, the match ending soon after at 290-458. ouch.


Down 0-2, I went into my third game facing Lisa Kessler. I had the first move, exchanging down to just the T from a rack of BGLNTTY. I drew well and bingoed with EDITION on my next turn. On my next move, I made a series of costly word knowledge blunders. I played ZING, stopping one before a triple letter score. I held the Y, believing that ZINGY was no good, while getting rid of it would have greatly improved my leave. Lisa responded by hooking the exact word I doubted, playing YIN and (ZING)Y for 40 points. I confidently challenged the play, and embarrassingly lost my turn when ZINGY came back valid. Lisa then played (R)ULELESS putting her up by 52 points. She managed to hold this point lead margin until I had the opportunity to play either AL(L)URERS or URET(E)RAL. Missing both, I instead played RUR(A)L, a blunder. I had one more winning chance after I spotted JEBE(L) for 44. Tragically I doubted the word in the critical moment, believing it could only be spelled DJEBEL, and that JEBEL was invalid. Instead I played J(A)B, a game losing blunder. The board quickly closed down and I was unable to land a comeback bingo despite holding a blank, leading to a 78 point loss.


Sitting with three losses and no wins, I felt my chances of placing slipping away. But no time to worry about that, I had to prepare to play against Morris Greenberg. I lost my game against him in the Toronto Invitational earlier this month, so I was looking to even the record. Little did I realize the shenanigans that were about to unfold.

Morris had first move, opening instantly with the bingo of GLISTEN for 70 points. Not looking good for me. I revealed my own tiles; AAAIIOO. Really not looking good for me. I exchanged tiles holding just the A, hoping for the best. Morris responded with his second bingo in a row, WHI(T)ENER for 65 points. Now I'm down 135-0. Really, really not looking good for me. My rack after exchanging was ACELMOY, which contained some heavy consonants. Amazingly, I had a playable bingo of CLAYMO(R)E (!!) for 80 points, which I played immediately upon spotting it. Morris commented on how great of a play it was, and I could not agree more.

I drew JQV out of the bag on my replenishment draw after bingoing, and spent several turns cleaning up my rack with QI followed by JO(I)N. After Morris' bingo using the first blank of TONETTE (a simple flute used to teach children), I drew the Z with no vowels to go with it, forcing my play of Z(E)ST for 46 holding HNTV. I also seriously considered V(E)NT for 22 holding HSTZ. Down by over 100, I panic opened with (O)VUM, which I followed up with (M)YNAH to keep scoring pace after failing to draw a bingo. I then drew the X, however there was a good spot for EX scoring 60 points. Now I'm only down by 34 points, and I saw winning as a realistic possibility. However, my dreams were shattered after Morris played DARIOL(E)S (a french cooking mold) using his second blank, sealing the game in his favor. In the end I scored 402 to Morris' 521, a ridiculous result. I also find it amusing how I drew every single power tile, ZQXJK and none of the "lowest scoring tiles" as in the blanks. Super fun.


Michael Fagen is the highest rated player I've ever gotten to play. He won the 31st National Championship with a 10000 dollar prize. By now, I had abandoned all hope of winning any prize money, and was not concerned with winning or losing against him, just playing my absolute best game. I went first and opened with PEEVE, the top option. Michael responded with TELLIES for 70 points. My next turn was tricky. I held DHIOOUX, an unappetizing rack. I took a few minutes to spot the best move of (T)OXOID for 32, holding HU. After some intermediate moves, I noticed that I had the crazy bingo of VIDEOF(I)T (!?) for 69 points, bingoing from an improbable rack. Only one problem; VIDEOFIT is only valid in the worldwide Collins lexicon, not the lexicon we were playing in. Michael held the play, but elected not to challenge the word (oh my goodness gracious). He later told me that the reason he hesitated was due to my 20-2 performance at Niagara, so he figured I had a lot of skill. I felt a great sense of pride in that a world class player as strong as Michael doubted himself playing against me, out of respect for my word knowledge. I was feeling pretty good at this point, despite Michaels double-double bingo of (C)YANIDES followed by his Z-bomb of ZA leaving me trailing by 91 points. In the critical moment, I found my only bingo of MARTINS (a species of songbird) for 83 points to even the score.

We both knew the next bingo would likely decide the game, and my opponent got to it before I did. When Michael finished laying down his tiles to play his bingo, I clasped my palms and became dizzy. I could see that he was about to bluff on me with the invalid but very plausible bingo of BRIDGER. In an incredible coincidence I had looked up the validity of this word many months ago, and knew with certainty it was phony. The word stuck with me all this time as it is a term used in a video game I used to play: Minecraft Bedwars. To get to other player's floating islands the player needs to build quickly over a void, and skilled players are known as "speedbridgers" or "godbridgers". I challenged the play off the board, and responded with my own bingo of HARKENS, using the first blank. I immediately picked the second blank out of the bag, securing victory on the spot. 463-416 was the final tally, and I couldn't have been happier to come away with a win against the national champion.



My next game against Ben Lam was a disaster, I genuinely have no clue what happened. I failed to spot many bingoes; CAPABLY, COEQUAL, LEUCOMA, COPULAE, CAGOULE, VACUOLE, EULACHON, EANLING, ANELING, ENLACING, NARGILEH, GERANIAL, NARGHILE, and INHOLDER. I also learned some new words; CARBARYL, CARBAMYL, and ACOELOUS. This was on five separate turns where I missed multiple possible bingoes each turn. 328-426, 1W-5L.


The next match against expert player Max Panitch did not go any better than my last. I spotted STARRIN(G) but instead played a phony that at the time, I was almost certain was good: RERATIN(G) (it didn't stay on the board). I later phonied again with (H)OUSABLE (it didn't stay on the board either). My valid bingo that turn was (H)AMULOSE. I finally bingoed with ALE(H)OUSE, and the game ended soon afterward. 287-438, 1W-6L.


The first day of the tournament was coming to a close. I had one more game against David Teresi, a friend of mine. We met at the University of Waterloo Scrabble Club, where we play many games of scrabble. David is an very fast player, and I have lost many games to him on the clock, some of which I accumulated -60 or -80 points in time penalties. I had clunky racks on my first three turns, which I bailed myself out of by playing my best moves of (R)AWIN, QI, and D(R)OPT. David played the phony 9 letter word (UN)CREASED, which looked valid enough for me to accept almost instantly. Turns out it's only valid in the international Collins dictionary. With a deficit of nearly 100 points, I bingoed in the nick of time with TEAMING for 88, hooking my M onto OI to form M(OI) in the triple word score lane. Next, I played JEED for 67, and drew the Z as the bag began to empty. I played defensive moves of CIG and ZA(P) to prevent any bingoes, and won the game 395-343. The result of this match meant that I was 2-6 after my first day of the tournament. Ah well, tomorrow is a new day.


Chapter 2 - It's Never Too Late

Six more games to go, starting off with my matchup against Hank Hees. He was very friendly, and was not peeved despite me showing up a few minutes late. We exchanged some friendly banter before the competition. I opened with HAVEN for 30, which Hank countered with ZON(A)TED for 68 points. Sometimes when a word comes onto the board, I immediately think of the "glaring hook"; IGNITES:LIGNITES, ACONITE:TACONITE, etc. In this game, the front O hook of ZONATED forming OZONATED never occurred to me for whatever bizarre reason. It didn't end up mattering much, but I was still surprised the moment I noticed it the day back from the tournament.

Later in the game with a rack of GGGIIIT, I played GIG for 23 rather than exchanging which I think was the correct decision. Hank responded with ABFARAD (an obsolete unit of capacitance) for 96, which is a word I had known following my revision of all the 7 letter bingos starting with an A. Still swamped by I's, my next play of DIGIT(A)L seems warranted. Hank bingoed again with CROUTES (a piece of toasted bread), and the board rapidly closed down. Just for the heck of it, I attempted SEMOLAS as an outbingo, which turned out to be invalid. I lost the game 309-529. After the game, Hank mentioned that he had been taking Mack Mellers scrabble lessons, which sounds like a lot of fun. I might need to get in on that. Five games remaining, 2-7.



My tenth game was a repeat vs David Teresi. I played by far the top equity move of JEE holding EEEIJLN, which opened the huge A(JEE) hook in the triple-triple lane. This may have been worth it, but I think it's very close. I whiffed ELAPINE on my next turn using my own A(JEE) hook, instead playing PLANE in the same spot. I then had the opportunity to make the cool play of Z(A)PTIEH (a Turkish policeman) for 68, but didn't know the word at the time. It can also be spelled ZAPTIAH. We exchanged bingoes, David playing WINTERY and me responding with HIDEOUT. The score was neck and neck up to this point, and I decided to burn my blank to play FLAY for 59 points on a closed board, and ended up winning 423-369. Four games left, 3-7.


My next repeat was vs Lisa Kessler. I phonied with SCOWLY, which was not challenged. I made a series of high scoring moves and despite not bingoing, found myself up by 50 points on a board that was rapidly constricting. I blocked each opening Lisa made, preventing any bingoes from her. I eventually won, 394-322. 4-7 with three games to go.


I wanted to prove my ability to win against Ben Lam, my next opponent whom I lost a game to on day one, where I played poorly. After my opening exchange, Ben played TOONIE, giving me the one tile I needed to play URETHA(N)E (synthetic resin), my only bingo. Ben responded with the invalid MOTORER, which I held and challenged off the board. I then bingoed in my only available spot for a second straight turn: LANKIER for 66. Ben bingoed with A(R)RANGES, but still trailed, and I extended my lead further by playing A(N)TIGENE. After my XI play for 52 in the endgame, I won 443-349. Now I'm on a winning streak with 5-7 and feeling confident.


Another game against William Barfuss. I could feel the momentum building in my favour after my play of LAW(N)Y for 39 which William challenged unsuccessfully, and my immediate bingo of SLITHER right after. I was up by 92, easy win. Nope, I(T)EMIZED followed by REQUIT(E)D from William brought him right back into the game. After my NIP, we were playing even at 190-190. I piled on the points with JO, GOX, CASING, and VOW. VOW was interesting to me. It opened the A(VOW) hook which would be hard to hit with the A in the right spot, but my leave of AEST meant I was very likely the one who would end up capitalizing on it. I felt it had to be correct, and it paid off. I drew into ASTERIA (a star-shaped figure formed by light reflected in a gem) hooking my spot for 85, putting me up by 94 points. Not even a bingo would be enough for William to win, right? Well... I played PUBL(I)C reasoning that no bingo could score enough. William then played ANESTRI (a period of sexual inactivity) between two columns in a 5-way overlap (!!), wedging his bingo into the crevasse in a tetris-like fashion for 85 points. I was praying in my head that it wouldn't be enough to win. And indeed it was not. My play of PUBL(I)C squeaked me ahead by just enough to win by 11 points! Incredible stuff. And the best part is, I was now 6-7 on a 4 game winning streak.


In my final game versus Lisa Kessler I opened with LUCK, which was quite fitting given my lucky streak. Lisa phonied with NEEDAB(L)E, which my brain automatically accepted as a word, although in hindsight it looks quite ridiculous. Able to be needed, that just doesn't sound right. Either way, I responded with (D)IXIT to rid myself of two I's which was lucky, given I drew another one immediately after. I played my best bingo of (F)ORESAIL for 86, and Lisa responded with GUSTO hooking DIXIT to form its plural of DIXITS. I held and ultimately decided to challenge DIXITS. I wrongly remembered the definition of DIXIT as being Latin for the interjection "I have spoken", therefore it would not take an S. However, I was thinking of DIXI, which is Collins only. DIXIT on the other hand is an "arbitrary dogmatic statement" (sure). All hope was not lost, I bingoed twice from there with DE(C)ANTER (a kind of glass container) and OSTIOLE (a mushroom pore where spores are released) to take a lead.

One more thing happened during this game that I found very interesting. I played PWNED which Lisa immediately challenged, thinking I had misspelled PAWNED. This gave me an extra turn to play GI(T)ANO and end the game, winning 427-348. To be fair, PWN is a completely ridiculous sounding word without context, and a new addition to the dictionary which threw off my long-time expert opponent who simply had not looked over the new words. It's interesting to me how changes in the way people communicate creates new words, which in turn changes the scrabble meta. And that's how scrabble gets its updates, in a way chess or poker could never.


Chapter 3 - The Aftermath

I had finished the Michael Wise Memorial tournament with a 7-7 record and a spread of -450. I did not realize it until the prize ceremony, but I had nearly won the class prize I had hoped to win from the start of day one, despite abandoning that possibility at the end of that same day. William Barfuss barely finished ahead of me, also going 7-7 but with a higher spread of -373 to my -450. Incredible how a small change in mindset perhaps could have pushed me to play just a bit better, and take home a prize. That's beautiful.

I saw many familiar friends winning in the award ceremony. In division 3, Raymond Zhu won with a shocking performance rating of 1399. It's astonishing to me how well Raymond has been performing in his recent tournaments, especially given his young age. I have played him across several online games, and his play is very consistently strong.
Caleb Pittman was the winner of division 2, beating out Evan Bialo on spread. I have played both Caleb and Evan across many games, and I think their skill levels are very similar. Their neck and neck finishes in this tourney helps to confirm my theory.
Morris Greenberg won division 1 with an incredible performance rating of 2143 (!!). A very skilled player for sure, I've now got two tournament losses against him. I have noticed that he is very eager to analyze endgames after the conclusion of a game. I think it's those habits that help you improve, always learning something after each game which I think is great.
